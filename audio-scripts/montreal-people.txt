Montreal: learn these people first.

A viewing guide to the core and the jobs around it.

Start with captain Nick Suzuki. A centre's value is easy to underestimate if you watch only the final touch before a goal. Follow Suzuki's support routes, how quickly he makes the next decision and where he positions himself when Montreal loses possession. A centre who makes his wings' jobs easier can affect an entire line without dominating the highlight reel. Number fourteen is your anchor when the game feels too fast.

Cole Caw field wears thirteen. The obvious skill to notice is finishing, but the interesting part often happens before the shot. Watch how he leaves a defender's attention, changes his angle and presents his stick as a target. A smaller scorer cannot simply stand in the same spot and expect the defence to forget him. Timing and deception create the fraction of a second in which a quick release becomes dangerous.

Juraj Slaf koff ski, number twenty, brings a different physical shape to the attack. Instead of equating size with hitting, watch puck protection, reach, board work and the ability to continue a play through contact. A big winger helps a skilled line if he turns a contested puck into a controlled pass. He does not need to win every collision in order to be useful. The better question is whether his physical advantages create time for his linemates.

Lane Hut son, number forty-eight, is the defenceman to watch when you want to see a defender make the first mistake without touching him. Look at the small changes of direction, the implied pass and the way he draws pressure. Every offensive defenceman also has to manage the space behind him. When Hut son attacks, look for the teammate covering his position. Evaluating the risk requires seeing that supporting structure as well as the skill.

Ivan Demidov is a young attacking talent to learn, with creativity as an obvious viewing question. Kirby Dach, Alex Newhook and Oliver Kapanen are useful names when thinking about support through the middle and the distribution of offensive responsibility. Phillip Danault and Jake Evans are worth watching for less spectacular centre work: faceoffs, defensive routes and difficult assignments. Josh Anderson and Chris Kreider give the group experienced physical options. The current roster has changed, so an old guide may still list players who have moved on.

Martin Saint Lou ee is the head coach in this edition. As a player, he reached the Hall of Fame despite being undrafted and undersized by traditional NHL expectations. That background makes development an interesting lens through which to watch his coaching, but a biography does not prove a system works. Judge the team by its spacing, decisions, special teams and whether young players become more complete. Kent Hughes is the general manager, a different role responsible for building the roster rather than choosing every shift.

Finally, learn the difference between a depth chart and a lineup. An injured player may appear on the official roster. A healthy player may be scratched, meaning not selected to dress. A prospect may be recalled for cover and never start. Goalie availability can change the entire evening. The app's injury cards and full roster are dated separately, and neither should be mistaken for a promise about who will be on the ice in a future game.
