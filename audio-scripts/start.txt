A way into the game, without homework anxiety.

Welcome to the Vibes Hockey Club. Five friends, five teams, and absolutely no requirement to justify how you chose yours. Picking a team because the name, colours, city or general emotional weather feels right is a perfectly respectable beginning. The useful next step is learning what you are looking at. This course starts from zero, then gives Montreal extra attention and introduces Minnesota, Pittsburgh, Ottawa and Seattle. You can listen in order or dip into a team guide.

The first thing to know is that hockey is a game about making time and space disappear. A player who has the puck wants enough of both to do something useful. The defending team wants to take them away. Skating, passing, checking and positioning are different tools for that same contest. A beautiful pass matters because it reaches somebody before the defenders can adjust. A good defensive play may look uneventful because it prevents the exciting thing from happening.

You do not need to see the puck continuously. Even experienced fans lose it. Watch the players' heads, the direction their sticks point and the camera movement. When a pass disappears behind the boards, look for the next player preparing to receive it. When the broadcast zooms in on a scrum, wait for the wider view before deciding who is open. A surprisingly useful habit is to watch the player who is about to get the puck rather than the player who has it now.

There are several kinds of enjoyment available. You can follow the score, follow a favourite player, appreciate tactical patterns, or care about the long story of a club. You can enjoy a fight scene in Shoresy and still prefer a clean zone exit in an NHL game. You can recognise that a player had a bad night without deciding that he is useless. A team can play well and lose; it can also win while playing a game its coach will dislike.

For your first few Canadiens games, pick three anchors: Nick Suzuki when Montreal has the puck, Lane Hut son when the defence starts an attack, and whichever goalie is actually starting. Add Cole Caw field once following three people becomes easy. The purpose is recognition, not memorisation. Soon you will notice who is on the ice before the commentator tells you.

The guide is a snapshot assembled on 15 October 2026. Roster and statistical tables come from official NHL data. Injury cards carry the date of the report, which may be earlier. Tactical watch points are explanations and questions, not secret knowledge of a coach's instructions. Current-season scores are hidden until you choose to reveal them; the audio course does not read out current-season results. Previous seasons and historical outcomes are discussed openly.

Something to try next time. During the next game, follow one player for an entire shift. Ask where he goes when he does not have the puck.
